# Research: a small, reliable macOS shell-command model

**Snapshot:** 2026-10-21. **Target:** Apple M4 MacBook Pro, 16 GiB unified memory, offline-first CLI, stock macOS with `/bin/zsh -f` and BSD utilities.  
**Decision:** Qwen3-1.7B is the **provisional first LoRA candidate**; LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct is the efficiency challenger; Qwen3.5-2B is the capability challenger. **No new model has yet been shown to beat the project baseline on independently adjudicated macOS requests.**

Execution checklist and commands: [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md).

> **Status, not a release decision.** This is a documentation-only model-selection proposal. Nothing here authorizes consuming `eval/frozen.jsonl` or `eval/heldout.jsonl`, changing the validator, executing generated commands, selecting a checkpoint from protected scores, training on unverified v4 labels, or replacing the shipped NL2SH model. The existing issues [#36](https://github.com/imaddde867/whatisit-macos/issues/36), [#15](https://github.com/imaddde867/whatisit-macos/issues/15), and [#11](https://github.com/imaddde867/whatisit-macos/issues/11) remain authoritative.

## Executive decision

Prefer a small, boring, fully controllable model to a more impressive generic model if the latter costs measurable command correctness, cold start, resident memory, reproducibility, or training feasibility. The first experiment should compare **pretrained+instruct** checkpoints on development requests *before* choosing where to spend verified training labels. Current ordering is a *research priority*, not a measured model-quality ranking:

| Priority | Candidate | Why test | Main risk | License |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — primary | [Qwen3-1.7B-MLX-4bit](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-MLX-4bit) | Dense Transformer, established MLX/LoRA path, usable non-thinking behavior, small checkpoint | Real macOS semantics unmeasured; exact thinking/template parity must be verified | Apache-2.0 |
| 2 — efficiency | [LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct-MLX-5bit](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct-MLX-5bit) | Smaller deployed weights, short outputs, promising published instruction/tool-use results | Hybrid backward performance and domain learning unmeasured; non-Apache license | LFM 1.0 |
| 3 — capability | [Qwen3.5-2B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B) | Stronger general reasoning on several vendor benchmarks | MLX Gated DeltaNet backward bottleneck; multimodal checkpoint handling; task accuracy unmeasured | Apache-2.0 |
| Control | Existing [NL2SH-1.5B](https://github.com/ThorOdinson246/whatisit-nl2sh) | Existing specialist, familiar production worker and measured errors | Low semantic reliability; upstream LoRA weights unavailable independently of shipped GGUF | Apache-2.0 stated; inspect upstream data notices |

The finalist may change after direct, matched zero-shot and training feasibility measurements.

The semantic audit examined **883** distinct (case, command) items; **148** labels changed versus regex matching (**66** false accepts, **82** false rejects). Student failures were concentrated in wrong output, missed constraints, hallucinated flags, wrong operands, wrong tools, and some Linux-only syntax. Not all model outputs were audited, and no full ground truth was established. **Do not compare new model scores from a different validator or scorer against historical percentages.**

### Why data quality precedes more tuning

- The [teacher ceiling study](../teacher-ceiling.md) attributed heavy label losses to exact-string majority voting: correct but differently spelled commands split votes; **41/141** teacher cases lacked a majority despite valid alternatives.
- The earlier full corpus dropped **1,207/2,520** requests as `no_majority`, while v3 recovered only 60. This is a high-leverage data problem, not proof of a model-capacity limit.
- v3 used **1,149** rows (902 commands, 247 abstentions), with **51/60** newly recovered labels judged correct by a model and eight wrong/one debatable. This is not a human-verified gold set.
- The [Learning64 verification audit](../learning64/verification-audit.md) exposed evaluator/fixture defects. Improvements in memorization or likelihood cannot stand in for semantic generalization.
- The new v4 pass-1 judgments are not a qualified training dataset until independent confirmation, a leakage audit, recorded quality controls, and reproducible build pass [#36](https://github.com/imaddde867/whatisit-macos/issues/36).

## 2. Candidate comparison, with evidence levels

Evidence levels: **MEASURED** = this repository/hardware; **VENDOR** = published by model author on other tasks; **IMPLEMENTATION** = source code or model artifact; **ESTIMATE** = planning hypothesis; **UNKNOWN** = must be tested.

### Qwen3-1.7B — first training candidate

- **IMPLEMENTATION:** official [MLX 4-bit checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-MLX-4bit), [original weights](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B); approximately **914 MB** published quantized model size; Apache-2.0.
- **IMPLEMENTATION:** dense Qwen3 Transformer; standard projection LoRA available via [MLX-LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/utils.py), with controls for rank, scale, adapted projection names and number of terminal layers.
- **RISK:** Qwen3 can produce thinking tokens. Compare **actual rendered training tokens** and serving tokens, explicit `enable_thinking` behavior, generation suffix, eos and loss mask. The official MLX conversion's tokenizer/template may differ from the original HF template; do not assume an unused keyword disables thinking.
- **UNKNOWN:** stock-macOS correctness, hallucinated BSD flags, abstention recall, compositional generalization, and true QLoRA optimizer memory on *this* M4.

### LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct — low-footprint challenger

- **IMPLEMENTATION:** official [MLX 5-bit checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct-MLX-5bit), **805 MB** published model size. Architecture mixes convolution and attention; MLX-LM has an [LFM2 implementation](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/models/lfm2.py).
- **VENDOR:** strong constrained-instruction/tool-use results, but comparisons with different prompts, datasets or judging procedures **cannot** be treated as matched macOS benchmarks.
- **RISK:** LoRA projection names/coverage differ from Qwen; inference support is not proof of efficient end-to-end backward+optimizer on M4 16 GiB. The **LFM 1.0** license is not Apache-2.0; review redistribution and revenue conditions before publishing a derivative.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether smaller size meaningfully improves complete warm/cold command latency versus a 1.7B dense model.

### Qwen3.5-2B — general-capability challenger

- **IMPLEMENTATION:** [model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B) documents a Qwen3.5 hybrid, Apache-2.0, originally published as an image/text model. Establish an actual text-only MLX or llama.cpp serving path before comparison.
- **VENDOR:** stronger on some broad knowledge/reasoning evaluations than Qwen3-1.7B, with nonuniform instruction-following results. These are **not** observed macOS correctness gains.
- **IMPLEMENTATION + FIELD REPORT:** [mlx-lm #1956](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/issues/1956) documents that `GatedDeltaNet` uses a non-kernel backward path and scaling cost with trained GDN layers. An M4 Pro/24-GB Qwen3.5-4B test required roughly 5–14 seconds per step depending on adapted layers. **Do not transfer these timings to the 2B/M4 as measured results**.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether a restricted adapter targeting appropriate attention projections retains enough adaptation benefit without prohibitive backward cost.

## 3. Architecture, memory and latency

Published **weight size is not runtime RSS**. Memory requirements include runtime allocations, tokenizer/process overhead, KV or recurrent states, adapter weights, optimizer states and MLX caches.

| Mode | Number | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen3 1.7B MLX 4-bit model | ~914 MB | **Published checkpoint size**, not process RSS |
| LFM2.5 1.2B MLX 5-bit model | ~805 MB | **Published checkpoint size**, not process RSS |
| Existing default peak server RSS | ~1.18 GB | **Measured** under older production workload |
| Existing 1.5B zero-update LoRA peak allocation | 9.48 GiB | **Measured** on M4; different, dequantized recipe |
| New Qwen3 4-bit LoRA allocation | ~5–11 GiB | **Estimate only**; test with real optimizer update, 256/512 tokens, memory pressure and swap |
| New candidate cold/warm p50/p95 | — | **Unknown** until measured in matched *complete CLI* path |

Dense attention may be simpler to train; convolutions/recurrent hybrids can use less inference state, but accelerator kernel maturity and backward support dominate training feasibility. MoE active parameters do not reduce total model storage and automatically confer no advantage for 20–40-token answers.

The existing `~0.22–0.24 s` model-backed warm medians use an initialized upstream worker. A fresh `mlx_lm.generate` process should **not** be compared to those as though both measured the same thing.

Benchmark **cold startup**, **warm end-to-end**, **first token**, **decode rate**, **peak whole process-tree RSS**, **idle memory**, and **unload behavior** individually, always through the final safety gate when claiming product latency.

## 4. Reliability and safety

The model predicts a candidate command, **never executes it**. The existing bounded lexer, `maclint`, upstream safety checks and deletion guard continue to constrain command output. None establishes semantic correctness. A valid `pmset`, `stat`, `mdfind`, `diskutil` or `awk` expression may answer the wrong question.

Semantic review must test:

1. BSD/macOS vs GNU option mismatches, tools absent from stock PATH, false assumptions about builtin vs external commands.
2. Exact operands, directory scope, output shape, ordering and preservation of user-specified constraints.
3. Unrequested deletions, hidden shell execution, side effects, missing/ambiguous inputs and ability to refuse.
4. Equally valid alternative commands rather than regex reference-string identity.
5. Safe scratch-fixture execution **only for explicitly allowlisted commands**, with state-changing or destructive candidates judged statically by vetted reviewers.

Do not add an unrestricted second LLM to the production path merely to judge the first model; that introduces a second fallible dependency and latency.

## 5. Fine-tuning recommendation

**After #36 passes**: start Qwen3 4-bit frozen-base LoRA, rank 8, scale 20, last 12 decoder blocks, `q_proj` and `v_proj` only, batch 1, seq length 512, AdamW LR `1e-5`, masked prompt, 20-step **optimizer feasibility test**, then at most one pre-registered full arm. This is a starting hypothesis, not an optimized recipe. See [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md) for the exact CLI and gating.

Require a tokenizer/template parity test first, including the thinking mode and empty/abstention encoding. Retain checksum receipts and per-case evidence for all seeds. **Do not infer semantic quality from validation loss**.

Prefer training with a published 4-bit MLX checkpoint first; compare the runtime adapter to its unfused base, then validate any fused or GGUF Q4/Q5/Q6 export independently. QLoRA keeps the quantized base frozen; quantization and fusion can change output semantics. Do not assume an MLX adapter can directly run on `llama-server`.

### Catastrophic forgetting and generalization

Watch held-out-by-family (development) failure rates, not just train loss. Preserve challenging queries in **tool+flag families**, compositional constraints, operand changes, paraphrases and abstention classes. Excessive epochs or exclusively narrow labels may increase brittle command memorization and false confidence. Use grouped validation; compare the untrained model, adapter and quantized deployment under identical prompts. If new tool families improve but old families regress, do not treat net gains alone as sufficient.

## 6. Metrics, targets and binding promotion policy

Definitions:

- **Correct-command rate:** independently adjudicated semantically correct *accepted* commands / answerable requests.
- **Accepted-wrong rate:** wrong accepted commands / **all** requests; also report wrong/accepted, to expose over-abstention.
- **Abstention recall:** correctly refused genuinely unsupported/unsafe requests / genuinely unsupported/unsafe requests.
- **False-abstention rate:** unnecessarily rejected requests / answerable requests.
- **End-to-end latency:** from CLI invocation/request to validated user-visible response; report p50/p95 cold and warm and separate model initialization.
- **Memory:** model server and whole process tree peak resident size, MLX peak allocation, swap delta and after-idle resident memory.

**Aspirational product quality objectives, *not* replacements for the pre-registered #11 rule:**

| Dimension | Research objective |
| --- | --- |
| Development semantic correct | ≥ **95/141** on the old-sized development workload, measured afresh under the same corrected evaluator and safety code |
| Development accepted-wrong | ≤ **20/141**; zero accepted high-severity unsafe outputs |
| Sealed held-out product target | ≥ **120/150** correct and ≤ **5/150** accepted-wrong, **only** at the final gate (never tune toward individual results) |
| Held-out abstention | Aim ≥ **19/20** on the existing abstention subset; a larger separately sealed unsafe set is required for meaningful reliability bounds |
| Warm latency | Aspirational p50 ≤ 350 ms, p95 ≤ 900 ms |
| Cold latency | Aspirational p50 ≤ 2 s |
| Serving memory | Aspirational peak whole-tree RSS ≤ 2 GB; idle unload ≤ 60 s |

The targets are hypotheses. The binding [#11 KEEP/SHELVE rule](https://github.com/imaddde867/whatisit-macos/issues/11) is unchanged: **exact one-sided paired sign test p ≤ 0.05 on pooled frozen+held-out (59+150); abstain-missed not increased; neither development split regresses; memory and latency stay within the default+noise budget.** After #35, **re-evaluate default and candidate with the same corrected worker/scorer**; historical `68/209` is not a matched baseline. One final candidate and a single gate; no score-shopping after failure. An aspirational absolute score alone does not authorize KEEP.

## 7. AFM 3 and redistribution tradeoffs

The locally exposed Apple AFM 3 Core Advanced system model may be a useful **zero-shot, OS-dependent comparator**. Its managed sparse architecture and 8K context do not confer third-party access to model weights, independent conversion, or reproducible user-controlled training. Apple's older [Foundation Models adapter toolkit](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/) has model/OS compatibility and a ≥32-GB-Mac training requirement; do not assume it supports AFM 3 on this 16-GB Mac. Establish current compatibility from Apple documentation before an AFM experiment. AFM is **not** the open-weight publication target.

For an Apache-2.0 model, preserve upstream LICENSE, copyright and notices and document fine-tuning/quantization changes. For LFM 1.0, review the full [license](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct-MLX-5bit/blob/main/LICENSE), including threshold-based commercial terms. Separate **model-weight licensing** from **training-data provenance**: the upstream NL2SH's data disclosures and attribution remain a provenance risk, documented in [finetune-feasibility.md](../finetune-feasibility.md). Do not claim the pretraining corpus is reproducible merely because weights are downloadable.

## 8. Explicit stop conditions

Stop or defer an arm when:

- It fails stock-macOS syntax/semantic smoke tests or cannot reliably emit a short final command/abstention.
- Its MLX backward + optimizer update crashes, swaps excessively or exceeds a recorded memory budget on M4 16 GiB.
- Its train-token versus serving-token templates diverge, especially thinking prefix and EOS handling.
- It improves development regex matches but not blinded semantic correctness, or raises accepted-wrong/unsafe cases.
- The adapted checkpoint improves but its actual deployable GGUF or runtime adapter loses the gain.
- It requires persistent model memory or materially slower latency for marginal/non-significant improvement.
- V4 judge confirmation, provenance, human sample, or evaluation leakage checks remain unresolved.
